### The bug distribution — read it as an oracle map, not a risk shift

51 of 58 bugs sit outside `ropus/`; only 7 target the codec crate. **The tempting conclusion — "the codec is mature, the shell around it is where risk now lives" — was refuted and I am not publishing it.** Three reasons, all evidenced:

1. **Effort was allocated per *area*, not per line.** `ropus/` is 87k lines of `src` and got 1 of 10 passes; `ropusplay/` is 199 lines and also got 1. Normalising `ropus/` to `ropus-tools-core`'s bug density would predict ~292 codec bugs, not 7. The counts are the schedule's output.
2. **The review explicitly excluded the DSP defect class.** `wrk_docs/2026.07.31 - CR - overnight ropus core review.md:93`: *"Not defects: fixed-point wrapping, narrowing, and C-shaped loops were not reported without a source-confirmed out-of-domain caller or parity mismatch."* A pass that runs nothing cannot produce a parity mismatch. The filter for the codec's dominant defect class was set to a condition the method made unreachable. Consistent with that, **none of the 7 `ropus/` bugs is an arithmetic finding** — all are packet plumbing, CTL dispatch, slice bounds, or reset bookkeeping.
3. **The project predicted these exact gaps twelve weeks ago.** All 21 rows of `wrk_docs/testing-oracle-matrix.md` are codec oracles; not one covers `ropus-tools-core`, the four CLIs, or `ropus-fb2k`. The corpus row, dated 2026-05-06, pre-declares it does not assert *"seek, output gain, pre-skip trimming, granule positions… the oracle locks the bitstream, not the player semantics."* The sweep's headline finds — `00034/35/36/38/42/43/46` — instantiate that list almost item for item.

**The defensible version:** the bugs cluster where the oracles are absent. That layer is genuinely under-defended and 37 of 58 findings ship to end users, so it is the right place to spend the next engineer-week. But nothing *shifted* — the gap was written down and dated on 2026-05-06. What changed on 07-31 is **observation coverage**, not risk location.
